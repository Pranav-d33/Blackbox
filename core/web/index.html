01The problem

An AI drone calls a panel clean. Months later it catches fire.

Four people need to know what the drone saw and decided. Today all four get the same thing: the contractor's own database, editable by the one party with the most reason to edit it.

  1. The ownerpaid for an inspection and wants proof it happened as reported
  2. The insureris asked to cover the loss and needs evidence it can rely on
  3. The panel makerfaces a warranty claim and wants to know when the crack appeared
  4. The contractorhas to defend its record without being taken at its word

02How it works

Every checkpoint, five steps.

  1. 1It holds.The camera flags a mark. The robot stops and takes a photo, fingerprinted and tied to a fresh nonce.
  2. 2It asks a sealed model.The photo goes to a vision model running inside a hardware enclave (TEE).
  3. 3The enclave answers and signs."Cracked cell, severity 2, go closer", signed by the enclave's key, echoing the nonce.
  4. 4The robot checks.Signer allowlisted? Signature covers these exact bytes? Nonce echoed? Otherwise it refuses.
  5. 5It acts and records.A robot-signed, hash-chained log entry, anchored on a public chain.

No answer, no motion. If the model can't be reached in time the drone flies home and the log says 0G unreachable, no decision made. It never guesses, and it resumes from the exact point it stopped.

03Try to break it

Built for the operator you can't trust.

Forge a decisionThe operator signs a fake "continue" with its own key.Refused: signer is not on the manifest allowlist
Flip the answerKeep the enclave's real signature, change what it said.Refused: TEE signature does not cover this answer
Edit the logLower a severity after the flight.Verifier: entry #2 was changed after the robot signed it
Cut the AIKill the link to the model mid-flight.Safe stop: 0G unreachable, no decision made

And the counterfactual: replay the same flight with every answer forced to "continue", and a second drone flies straight past the damage. The model's answer is what moved the robot.

06SDK

Any robot. Three calls.

Implement Robot, write a mission profile (one JSON file: what the AI may answer, what the robot may do), and every decision is sealed, signed and logged. Solar farms and bridge decks are two profiles on the same code. A pipeline weld or a turbine blade is another file.

The input doesn't have to be a camera. A crew member can type or say what they see, and it goes through the same sealed model and the same interlock.

07The stack

Why a chain, and why these.

The parties don't trust each other, and the one running the robot has the most reason to edit the record. A signed log proves a file wasn't edited. It can't prove which model decided, that the answer was made for this checkpoint, or that the robot did only what it was told. A public chain gives everyone a commitment no one can quietly rewrite, checkable from a phone with no account.

0G ComputeDecides.Sealed inference in a TEE. The enclave's signature is what the robot checks before it moves.
0G StorageRemembers.The manifest, every robot-signed entry and the evidence, by content root. Crops are encrypted.
0G ChainCommits.Mission registered before takeoff, anchored every two decisions, closed with the trajectory hash.
ArkivAnswers questions.Storage returns a file by its root, nothing else. Arkiv makes each finding queryable by panel, maker and batch, owned by the wallet that wrote it. Never trusted for the verdict.
